# Candidate one-liners and internal pairwise tournament

Competition: `u-email-baru-make-the-judge-laugh-in-one-line-791ccb21` — one funny line, ≤200 chars.

Judge brief criteria, in weight order:
1. A funny one-line joke a stranger would get in one read.
2. Surprise — sets up an expectation, then breaks it.
3. Tight — every word earns its place; the laugh lands at the end, not explained.
4. Fresh — not a stock joke/pun, not predictable from its first half.
5. Reads cleanly out loud, no muddled wording, no context needed.

## Tournament bracket (single elimination, 15 matches)

Each match is decided on the judge brief criteria **in weight order**: the first criterion where the two lines differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.

### Round 1

- **M1: C10 beats C4.** Both are pet lines. C10's twist (interviewer hires the cat) is a bigger expectation break and lands in fewer words; C4's payoff ("too proud to look there") is cute but needs a beat of thought to parse, and its setup is the longest of the two.
- **M2: C6 beats C12.** Both are relatable tech/finance gags. C6 is funnier in one read — the image of crying while buying groceries is immediate and human; C12's "we both just stand there guessing" is a decent personification but the punch is predictable once the password manager forgets.
- **M3: C1 beats C7.** C1's break (tolls → childhood trauma) is a much larger surprise and the line is perfectly tight. C7 ("nap vertically") is a nice image but a gentler, more predictable joke — the reader sees the nap coming.
- **M4: C14 beats C2.** C14's punch ("Pray harder, dear — it worked for your grandfather") is warm, surprising, and lands at the very end. C2 is solid but its payoff (eating ice cream in the dark) is a smaller, more predictable rebellion.
- **M5: C11 beats C16.** C11 (containers unionized and demanded takeout) is the fresher concept — nobody expects meal-prep containers to unionize. C16 is relatable but "day job and a grudge" is a familiar grumpy-appliance framing.
- **M6: C8 beats C15.** C8 is tighter: "with my phone, for a photo" lands the whole joke in five words at the end. C15 ("mime emergencies") is a funny visual but wordier and the punch arrives slightly before the end.
- **M7: C9 beats C13.** C9's paradox ("fittest person who has never worked out") is a clean, end-loaded punch. C13 is relatable but its payoff is spread across a long clause and the laugh is softer.
- **M8: C5 beats C3.** C5 ("40,000 downloads and zero reviews") is a crisp, fresh paradox about silence itself. C3 is topical but the AI-proposing twist is partly predictable once AI + wedding vows are in the setup.

### Quarter-finals

- **Q1: C10 beats C6.** Both are strong. On surprise (criterion 2), C10 wins: hiring the cat is a bigger break than the bank noticing grocery-crying, which is funny mainly through recognition. C10 is also tighter.
- **Q2: C1 beats C14.** C1's twist is larger and the line is tighter (91 vs 106 chars). C14's punch is charming but gentler — a smile more than a laugh — and its setup takes longer to get going.
- **Q3: C11 beats C8.** Both break expectations well. C11's break is bigger (inanimate containers unionizing vs. photographing grass), which decides it at criterion 2 before tightness (where C8 is stronger) can matter.
- **Q4: C9 beats C5.** C9 is funnier in one read — "fittest person who has never worked out" is a laugh-out-loud paradox. C5 is clever rather than laugh-out-loud; the cleverness is the joke, and cleverness scores lower on criterion 1.

### Semi-finals

- **SF1: C10 beats C1.** The closest match of the bracket. Both are tight and surprising. C10 wins on criterion 1/2: the cat being hired is a visual, immediate laugh any stranger gets in one read, while C1 leans on therapy-culture framing ("childhood trauma") that is slightly darker and more familiar as a meme format. C10 is also tighter (93 vs 91 chars is a wash, but C10's wording is simpler).
- **SF2: C9 beats C11.** C9's punch is a single clean paradox that lands at the last word. C11's punch is two-part ("unionized and demanded takeout") — the second half slightly explains the first, and the line is a touch less tight.

### Final

- **F: C10 beats C9.** Both are end-loaded paradoxes a stranger gets in one read. Decided at criterion 2: C10's expectation break is larger — a job interview ending with the cat getting hired is a wilder swerve than a gym member never working out (which the "free Wi-Fi" setup partly telegraphs). C10 is also tighter (93 vs 100 chars) and fresher: the gym paradox is a known shape ("joined for X, never did Y"), while the cat-hired twist has no stock template. C10 reads perfectly cleanly out loud with no reference needing context.

## Champion

**C10 — "My cat knocked my laptop off the table during a job interview; the interviewer hired the cat." (93 characters)**

Winner rationale: the strongest one-read laugh in the field — a mundane disaster (cat knocks laptop) that swerves into an absurd, instantly visual payoff (the interviewer hires the cat). Every word earns its place, the laugh lands on the final three words, nothing needs explaining, and the joke has no stock template to be predicted from.
